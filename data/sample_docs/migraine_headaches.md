# Clinical Guide: Headaches and Migraines

## Overview
Headaches are categorized into primary (e.g., tension-type, migraine, cluster) and secondary (resulting from underlying pathology like infection, trauma, or vascular disease).

## Migraine Characteristics
- Unilateral, pulsating or throbbing quality of moderate to severe intensity.
- Aggravated by routine physical activity (walking, climbing stairs).
- Accompanied by nausea, vomiting, photophobia (sensitivity to light), or phonophobia (sensitivity to sound).
- May have an "aura" (visual disturbances such as flashing lights, zigzag lines, or blind spots) occurring 20–60 minutes before headache onset.

## Migraine Management Strategies
- Identifying and avoiding specific individual triggers (sleep deprivation, dehydration, skipping meals, aged cheeses, nitrates, stress).
- Resting in a dark, quiet room with cold compress during an episode.
- Hydration and electrolyte balance.
- Preventative vs acute abortive therapies (educational: triptans, NSAIDs, CGRP antagonists).

## Headache Red Flags (The SNOOP Criteria)
Seek emergency medical attention if a headache presents with:
- **Systemic symptoms:** Fever, weight loss, stiff neck.
- **Neurologic signs:** Confusion, impaired alertness, weakness, numbness, or seizures.
- **Onset:** Sudden "thunderclap" headache reaching maximal intensity within seconds or minutes (suggestive of subarachnoid hemorrhage).
- **Older age:** New headache onset after age 50.
- **Pattern change:** Progressive headache that worsens with coughing, straining, or positional changes.
