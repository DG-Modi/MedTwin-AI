# Clinical Guide: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

## Overview
Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, leading to chronic hyperglycemia.

## Key Symptoms
- Increased thirst (polydipsia) and frequent urination (polyuria)
- Unintended weight loss and increased hunger (polyphagia)
- Persistent fatigue and lack of energy
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores or cuts
- Frequent infections (e.g., gum, skin, or vaginal infections)

## Management Principles
1. **Lifestyle and Nutrition:**
   - Balanced Mediterranean or low-glycemic dietary pattern rich in whole grains, lean proteins, vegetables, and fiber.
   - Reduction of refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages.
   - At least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week combined with resistance training.
2. **Blood Glucose Monitoring:**
   - Routine self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) or continuous glucose monitoring (CGM).
   - Target HbA1c is generally below 7.0% for most non-pregnant adults, individualized based on age, hypoglycemia risk, and comorbidities.
3. **Common Pharmacological Classes (Educational Overview):**
   - Biguanides (e.g., Metformin as first-line therapy unless contraindicated).
   - SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists (beneficial for cardiorenal risk reduction).
   - Sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, and Insulins.

## Acute Complications and Red Flags
- **Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar State (HHS) or Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** Symptoms include extreme thirst, confusion, vomiting, deep rapid breathing, or fruity-smelling breath. Requires urgent emergency medical evaluation.
- **Severe Hypoglycemia:** Blood sugar below 54 mg/dL with confusion, sweating, tremors, or loss of consciousness. Requires immediate fast-acting carbohydrates (Rule of 15) and medical assistance if unresponsive.
